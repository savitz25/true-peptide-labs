Storage and Stability of Lyophilized Peptides

General lab-oriented notes for handling lyophilized research peptides. This page does not invent shelf-life or stability data for VialVibes lots.

Author: VialVibes research documentation team Reviewer: — (slot reserved) Last reviewed: 2026-07-28

Scope and honesty about data

Stability of a peptide depends on sequence, salt form, residual moisture, packaging, temperature history, light exposure, and laboratory handling after opening. VialVibes does not publish invented expiration dates, accelerated stability studies, or lot-specific shelf-life claims on this educational page.

Follow product documentation when provided, your institution’s SOPs, and lot CoA notes. When in doubt, contact research@vialvibes.com about available documentation for a specific product or lot.

General laboratory storage of lyophilized material

Many laboratories store unopened lyophilized peptides under conditions that reduce heat, moisture, and light exposure. Common practice themes include:

  • Cool, controlled temperature: Refrigerated storage is frequently used for research peptides; some labs use freezer storage depending on compound and institutional SOP. Confirm against any product-specific guidance you receive.
  • Protect from moisture: Lyophilized cakes can absorb water. Keep containers tightly closed; minimize open-vial time in humid environments; consider desiccants only as allowed by your lab SOPs.
  • Protect from light: When light-sensitive species are a concern, store in opaque containers or dark cabinets as appropriate for the material.
  • Keep sealed until use: Leave original seals intact until the vial is needed for reconstitution or aliquot preparation.

After opening and after reconstitution

Once a vial is opened or material is reconstituted, degradation risk profiles change. Laboratories often:

  • Document open dates and reconstitution solvent/volume in lab notebooks;
  • Prepare single-use aliquots to reduce freeze–thaw cycles when solutions will be stored;
  • Store solutions under conditions defined by internal method development rather than assumptions from dry powder practice.

For laboratory reconstitution technique context (not human dosing), see Reconstitution Best Practices and the reconstitution calculator.

Practical receiving tips

  • Inspect packaging on receipt and record lot numbers for CoA matching.
  • Move material promptly into designated cold storage if that is your SOP.
  • Request lot documentation via CoA lookup when required by your quality system.
