What happens next

  1. Send the lot details

    Identify the material, its lot, and an email for follow-up. A confirmation appears after successful submission.

  2. Documentation is checked

    The research desk checks for a report matching the requested lot. A document or a particular test may be unavailable.

  3. Review the follow-up

    Documentation is shared by email when available. Further label details may be needed to identify the lot.

  4. Match the report to your records

    Compare the material name and lot identifier, then review the methods, dates, results, and limitations.

Why the lot matters

A CoA records analytical information for a defined sample or batch. A report for another lot cannot establish the results for the material in your lab.

Matching the label and report helps maintain traceable laboratory records and interpret experiments alongside the actual test methods.

Can’t find the lot?

Check the vial label and outer packaging. If the identifier is missing or unclear, email hello@asktrusthub.com with the material name and available label details.

Read the report in context

What a CoA typically contains

This is an educational field guide, not a certificate for a live lot. Report formats and tests vary. Read only the results and methods actually included in the document you receive.

Material identity and lot
The reported compound name, form when stated, and batch identifier. Compare them with the label and your internal records.
Analytical methods
How the sample was examined, including the methods and conditions stated by the laboratory. Learn how HPLC and MS differ.
Identity and purity results
Separate findings supported by the tests reported. A purity percentage is method-dependent and does not, by itself, establish identity or suitability for a particular experiment.
Dates, laboratory, and limitations
Who issued the report, the analysis or report date, and any sample, method, or reporting limitations. Check these before filing the document.

A CoA does not establish suitability for human or veterinary use. Tests such as sterility or endotoxin testing should not be assumed unless explicitly reported. Read the limits of purity claims.
